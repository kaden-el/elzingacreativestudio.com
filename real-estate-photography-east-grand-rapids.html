Photography built for East Grand Rapids homes

East Grand Rapids is one of the most photographed real estate markets in West Michigan — and one of the most poorly photographed. The neighborhood has the architectural character that demands real twilight (Tudor revivals, mid-century brick, lakefront moderns around Reeds Lake) and the buyer pool that expects MLS galleries to look like a magazine. Most listings underperform their potential because the photographer treated a $700K East GR home the same way they treat a starter ranch in Wyoming.

Elzinga Creative Studio shoots East Grand Rapids listings with a different shot list. Real twilight at Reeds Lake-adjacent properties shows water reflections you cannot fake. Architectural detail shots on the Gaslight-area homes pick up the historic millwork buyers actually pay for. And drone aerials reveal how the lots sit in the East GR street grid in a way listings without aerials never communicate.

Why East GR listings need real twilight

The East Grand Rapids buyer pool skews higher income and more design-aware than the broader Grand Rapids market. They are scrolling Zillow on a tablet at 9pm and they have seen what real twilight looks like on listings in Ann Arbor, Birmingham, and Holland. A virtual-twilight East GR listing reads as "trying too hard." A real twilight East GR listing reads as "this is the home." For the deep dive on real vs virtual twilight, see our breakdown or the dedicated real twilight page.

Drone in East Grand Rapids — Part 107 + airspace

East Grand Rapids sits inside the Gerald R. Ford International Airport's controlled airspace, which means commercial drone work requires LAANC authorization on top of FAA Part 107 certification. ECS works under a Part 107 RPIC structure with proper airspace authorizations for all East GR flights. For the full breakdown on drone laws and what realtors should ask their photographer, see drone laws for Grand Rapids real estate agents.
