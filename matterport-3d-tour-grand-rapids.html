A 3D tour qualifies buyers before they walk in

Either outcome is a win for your seller because the showings you do get are warmer and the ones you skip would have been a waste of everyone's Saturday.

What a 3D tour is and why it works

A 3D tour is an interactive, navigable model of a home, not a video and not a slideshow. We capture overlapping scans of every space and stitch them into a single experience the buyer controls: they click to move forward, turn to look anywhere, and step into any room in any order. Because they are driving, they answer their own questions about layout, sightlines, and how the kitchen relates to the living room, the things flat photos can imply but never quite prove.

The reason it works is simple: it removes uncertainty before the showing. For an out-of-town or relocation buyer who cannot easily drive by, the 3D tour is the difference between a listing they trust and one they scroll past. A family moving to Grand Rapids for work can walk ten homes in an evening from a hotel room and arrive at their first real showing already knowing which three are worth their time. For local buyers, it filters out the homes that photographed well but do not fit, which means fewer wasted showings and a seller whose house is only opened up for people who are genuinely interested. A home with a tour signals that the listing is serious, and that impression carries.

Zillow 3D Home vs. Matterport: when each makes sense

Both produce an interactive walkthrough, but they are not the same tool, and the right pick depends on where your buyers are and what the listing is worth. Zillow 3D Home is free to host and earns a 3D Home badge plus better placement inside Zillow's own search, which matters because for most West Michigan listings that is where the bulk of buyer traffic already lives. It is the practical default for everyday homes that compete on Zillow visibility.

A Matterport-style tour produces a sharper, more polished result: a cleaner dollhouse view, more accurate spatial feel, and a premium presentation that travels well across the MLS, your own website, and email rather than living inside one platform. That makes it the better fit for higher-end and luxury listings, and for relocation-heavy properties where the tour is doing real selling work for a buyer who cannot visit. The Matterport-style walkthrough is part of the $100 add-on; a premium, fully-hosted Matterport Pro tour is $250 to $295, custom-quoted to the property. We shoot both, and on most listings the call is straightforward once you weigh the price point against where the buyers are. For a deeper side-by-side, see Zillow 3D Home vs. Matterport in Grand Rapids. When the listing calls for motion as well, a cinematic video walkthrough complements the self-guided 3D tour, and on premium properties the full luxury package pairs the tour with magazine-grade stills.
