The lakeshore without the lakeshore price

Nunica sits in Crockery Township in northern Ottawa County, near the junction of I-96, M-104, and M-231, with Crockery Creek and the Grand River flats nearby. Its appeal is location: a buyer here gets acreage, woods, and a quiet country setting while sitting just minutes from Spring Lake and Grand Haven and a straight shot to Grand Rapids or Muskegon. The listings run from affordable in-town homes to acreage and country properties. The whole pitch of a Nunica home is what surrounds it and what it is close to — the land, the setting, and the lakeshore right down the road — and none of that comes through in a phone photo of the kitchen.

Drone sells the setting and the location

On a Nunica listing, the drone aerial does double duty. It shows the property itself — the lot size, the outbuildings, the woods or creek — and it shows the thing buyers really care about here: how close the home sits to the lakeshore and the highways. An aerial that puts the property in its setting is often the image that makes a buyer realize they can have land and the coast within minutes. See the guide to real estate drone photography; every flight is by an FAA Part 107 certified pilot.

Interiors still have to hold the view

A country home near the lakeshore lives on its light and its windows. Shoot the interior flat and the windows blow out, hiding the woods, the creek, or the yard that is part of the home's appeal. Proper HDR holds both the bright room and the view through the glass, and keeps the natural light honest. That is the difference between a listing that feels like a country home and one that looks like any house. See flambient vs HDR real estate photography, and for premium properties, the luxury real estate photography approach.
