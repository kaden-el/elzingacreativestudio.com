Should You Rake Leaves Before Your Grand Rapids Listing Photos?

Kaden Elzinga October 7, 2026  ·  4 min read

I pulled up to a listing in East Grand Rapids last October and the front lawn was gone. Not dead, just buried under a thick layer of maple leaves. The seller had raked twice that week and the wind had undone it both times. That one yard is the whole fall question for real estate photography in Grand Rapids: rake, or leave it?

Why leaves matter more in photos than in person

Standing in the yard, a few leaves read as seasonal. In a wide-angle photo they read as neglect. The camera flattens everything, so a scattered lawn looks like a yard nobody has touched in a month.

Buyers scrolling listings decide in a second or two. A lawn that looks unkept makes them wonder what else was skipped inside.

The leaves I tell sellers to leave alone

A few leaves on a walkway or porch step are fine, and sometimes help. Color on a tree still holding its leaves is an asset, and I will shoot around it when the light is right.

What I won't fight is a handful of fresh leaves landing mid-shoot. I can clean those up in editing, and I tell sellers not to chase them.

The leaves that need to go before I arrive

Clear the driveway, the walkway to the front door, the porch, and any steps. Those are the paths a buyer walks, and they are in nearly every exterior frame.

Then clear the lawn directly in front of the house. Blowing leaves into a tidy pile at the edge, out of frame, is a short job on most lots and changes the whole exterior shot.

Why I do not just edit them all out

Editing can remove a few stray leaves. It cannot honestly rebuild a lawn that is covered edge to edge, and I would not want to. Buyers who see a perfect lawn in the photos and a leaf blanket at the showing feel misled, and that costs you trust at the worst moment.

Clean ground in the photos that matches the yard on showing day is what keeps the listing honest and the showings productive.

Gutters, window wells, and the patio

Leaves pile up where you do not look. Check the gutters, window wells, and any patio or deck you want in the photos.

A deck covered in wet leaves is one of the few things I cannot fully fix later. If the backyard matters to the listing, sweep it first.

Where to put the leaves

Bag them or move them out of sight before I arrive. If you live inside the city, Grand Rapids runs a yard waste program, and the city says service continues through the second Friday in December. Check the City of Grand Rapids yard waste page before you plan your cleanup.

Timing the shoot around the drop

West Michigan trees do not drop their leaves all at once. Oaks hang on late and maples let go fast, so a yard can look fine on Monday and buried by Friday.

Book the shoot as close to your cleanup as you can, and tell me if wind is in the forecast. Fall also gives us less daylight, so I plan exteriors for the brightest part of the day. If rain moves the date, my rain policy covers it.

What if the leaves come back after the shoot

They will, because it is October in Michigan. You do not need a spotless yard for the whole listing, only for the shoot. After the photos are taken, a quick pass before each showing is enough to keep the property looking cared for.

What I do on shoot day

I walk the exterior first and flag anything still in frame. If a few leaves landed since you raked, I handle them in editing. Delivery is next business day, so a small fix never slows your listing down.

If you are on the fence about whether your yard is ready, send me a phone photo before the shoot and I will tell you honestly what needs doing.

Fall listings in Grand Rapids can look great, and the leaves are usually a quick fix, not a reason to wait. Text me a picture of your front yard and I will tell you what I would do.
