Snow on Shoot Day: What to Do Before Your Grand Rapids Listing Photos

Kaden Elzinga October 21, 2026  ·  4 min read

It is 7 a.m. in Grand Rapids and the forecast was wrong. Four inches fell overnight, the photographer shows up at 10, and the agent texts me: "Should we cancel?" Most of the time, the answer is no. But what happens in the next two hours decides whether the photos look crisp or look like a mess.

I shoot real estate photography across Kent and Ottawa counties all winter, and snow day shoots are some of the most avoidable headaches I see. Here is what actually matters.

Shovel the path, not the whole yard

Clean, even snow on a lawn looks great on camera. It reads as quiet and tidy. What does not look great is a buried front walk, a half-cleared driveway, and boot tracks leading to the door.

Clear the driveway and the front walk down to the pavement, and edge them cleanly. Leave the lawn alone. A straight, dark line of clear concrete against white snow gives the exterior shot a shape it would not have in July.

Salt after shoveling, not before

Rock salt melts snow into slush, and slush photographs like a puddle. If the walk needs salt for safety, put it down after I leave. If you have to salt before, shovel first and keep it light so the pavement dries instead of turning gray and wet.

Safety comes first. I will always take a salted walkway over a slip hazard for the agent, the seller, or me.

Let the snow sit if it is clean

Fresh snow on the roof and trees is an asset. Dirty, plowed snow piled against the curb is not. If the plow left a ridge at the end of your driveway, break it down and push the pile off to the side, out of the frame.

Patchy, melting snow with brown grass poking through is the one condition I would call ugly. If a thaw has left the yard half white and half mud, a clean exterior is hard to get, and I will often shoot the interior first and revisit the outside later.

Turn on every light you own

Gray winter skies make interiors dark, and a grayer room makes a smaller room. This is the biggest difference between a summer shoot and a January one. Turn on lamps, overhead lights, under-cabinet lights, and the porch light before I arrive.

The warm glow in the windows also does real work in an exterior shot against a snowy yard. A house with the lights on looks lived in and welcoming. A house with the lights off looks closed.

Wet floors and salt tracks

Snow comes in on boots. Set a mat and a tray at the door, and ask everyone, including me, to leave shoes by the entry. Dried salt rings on hardwood show up under the camera even when you cannot see them in person.

Wipe the entry floors right before the shoot, then keep the door traffic low. It takes ten minutes and saves a lot of retouching.

When rescheduling is the right call

I reschedule when the roads are unsafe or when a storm is still dropping snow at shoot time, because falling snow shows up as specks across every exterior frame. For current conditions and forecasts, I check the National Weather Service office in Grand Rapids (weather.gov/grr) the night before and again that morning.

Travel is free within roughly 25 miles of downtown Grand Rapids, so a one-day slide costs you nothing extra. Photos still arrive the next business day after the shoot. Starting a listing on day one with better photos beats starting it a day sooner with muddy ones.

Use the early dark to your advantage

Winter sunsets arrive early in West Michigan, which makes a twilight exterior easier to schedule than in summer. A real twilight shot over clean snow is one of the best images a winter listing can have. It is $150 on its own and included in the Showcase and Signature packages.

If snow hits the night before and the yard looks perfect, ask whether we can move the exterior to dusk and keep the interiors for the morning.

Snow does not have to cost you a week. Shovel the walk, flip on the lights, and tell me what the yard looks like. I would rather adjust the plan at 7 a.m. than reshoot a listing after it has been live for a week. Text me at (616) 258-4578 and we will sort it out.
