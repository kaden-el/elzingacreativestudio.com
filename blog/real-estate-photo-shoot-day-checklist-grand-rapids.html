The Real Estate Photo Shoot Day Checklist for Grand Rapids Agents

Kaden Elzinga June 16, 2026  ·  6 min read

I have walked into listing shoots in Grand Rapids where the seller did not know I was coming, where every counter was covered, where the garage door was blocked by a car with a flat tire, and where the back deck had a half-assembled patio set on it. None of that is unusual. It is entirely preventable. The agents who get consistently great galleries have a process: they send a prep sheet, they manage the timeline, and they know what to expect on delivery. This is the full shoot-day checklist I would give every Grand Rapids agent I work with, from booking through going live on the MLS.

Two to five days out: booking and confirming the add-ons

Book the shoot with enough lead time to build in a reschedule if the weather turns. For most of the year in West Michigan, two to five days is enough. April through June, when the calendar fills fast, I recommend a week. Twilight and drone require a weather window and 5 to 7 days of lead time to do well. Book those when you confirm the listing, not the day before.

When you book, decide on the package and add-ons at that point — not the morning of the shoot. The standard packages at ECS are Essential ($245), Showcase ($375, includes real twilight and floor plan), and Signature ($525, includes everything plus drone). Add-ons like a 3D tour ($100), additional twilight ($150 on Essential), or agent reels ($150/$300) are best confirmed upfront. The photographer will plan the shoot schedule around what is booked. Deciding at the door costs time and sometimes the light.

Three days before: send the seller your prep checklist

This is the single most important thing most agents skip. A well-prepared home takes 90 minutes to shoot. An unprepared home can take two to three hours, delays the editing queue, and results in photos that need more work — or that simply look worse. Send the seller a concrete checklist, not a vague "make sure it's clean." The full version is on the how to prepare a home for real estate photos page, but the core items are:

  • Clear all counters — kitchen, bathrooms, laundry room. Leave only intentional decor.
  • Remove personal photos from walls and surfaces.
  • Hide all cords, cables, chargers, and small appliances.
  • Make all beds and hide decorative pillows that look chaotic.
  • Clear the driveway completely — all cars out before the photographer arrives.
  • Remove trash cans, hoses, and lawn equipment from the yard and driveway.
  • Mow and edge the grass within two days of the shoot.
  • Turn on all lights inside (including under-cabinet, closet, and accent lights) and replace any burned-out bulbs.

Sellers who get this list three days out have time to declutter without panic. Sellers who get it the morning of the shoot do not.

Shoot day: what actually happens and when to be there

You do not need to stay for the full shoot. I will call if there is a judgment call to make — a piece of furniture that should move, an exterior area that looks better with or without staging, a decision on whether to include a certain room. Being available by phone covers that.

What I do recommend is a walk-through at the start. Fifteen minutes at the beginning of the shoot to confirm what rooms are in scope, note anything the seller wanted highlighted or avoided, and confirm the exterior plan if twilight or drone is on the schedule. That 15 minutes prevents the most common post-shoot conversations. The seller, on the other hand, should not be home. They slow things down, they hover, and sometimes they talk the photographer out of a better shot by explaining why a room "usually looks different." The photographer needs to work the home as it is, not as it is being narrated.

Shoot timing depends on the package. Interiors take about 60 to 90 minutes for most homes. Twilight is a separate session, typically starting 30 to 45 minutes before official sunset, which the photographer will schedule based on the date. In Grand Rapids in midsummer that can be 8:30pm; in December it can be 4:30pm. Plan around that. Drone is typically scheduled in the same session as the main shoot for efficiency, and it adds 30 to 45 minutes depending on the property size. The full walk-through for a Signature shoot runs roughly 2.5 to 3 hours on site across the photo, drone, and twilight windows.

What the 24-hour delivery actually means

ECS delivers all standard galleries the next day, which means if the shoot is Tuesday at 10am, the images are back by Wednesday morning. Not "by end of week." Not "within 48 hours." Next day. The gallery arrives as a download link with all MLS-ready JPGs named and organized. For agents on a tight list-date timeline, same-day rush is available for an additional fee when a listing needs to go live the following morning before the standard overnight window closes.

What the 24-hour window does not include is going back for a reshoot. If the prep was incomplete and critical rooms were cluttered, the photos will reflect that. I can crop around some issues in editing, but I cannot fix a kitchen counter full of stuff in post. The prep checklist is not optional; it is the other half of the delivery guarantee.

After delivery: uploading to MLS and using the assets

The West Michigan MLS (GRAR MLS) has specific requirements for listing photo file size, resolution, and count. The short version: JPGs at 72 to 300 DPI, typically 10 to 25 photos per listing, with the exterior as the first image. The full breakdown is in the West Michigan MLS photo requirements guide. ECS galleries are pre-named and pre-sized to upload cleanly; you should not need to resize or convert anything.

Beyond the MLS, the same gallery goes to work in several places: your email blast, a social post with a selection of hero shots, and any print marketing pieces you use. If you have social cuts from the Showcase or Signature package, those are sized for Instagram and Facebook already. The floor plan from Showcase can go directly into print and digital flyers. The listing reel from Signature goes to your Instagram Reels, TikTok, and the video field on the MLS listing. Every asset in the package is built to pull double duty.

The version for your sellers

The checklist above is written for agents. The seller-facing version — what they need to do to their home before the photographer arrives — is a different document with a different tone, and it is the one worth forwarding. Everything is covered in the prepare-your-home guide. I use it as the template for the prep sheet I recommend agents send to sellers three days before every shoot. Feel free to copy it.

Frequently asked questions

Should the agent or seller be present during a real estate photo shoot? Sellers generally should not be home — they tend to hover and slow the shoot. The agent does not need to stay for the whole session either, but being available by phone is useful if the photographer needs to make a call about staging or an exterior detail.

What is the biggest mistake agents make before a real estate photo shoot? Not sending the seller a prep checklist in advance. A home where counters are clear, beds are made, and cars are out of the driveway shoots in 90 minutes. An unprepared home takes twice as long and produces a weaker gallery.

How far in advance should I book a real estate photo shoot in Grand Rapids? Two to five days works for most of the year. Spring (April through June) fills faster — book a week out. Twilight and drone add-ons need 5 to 7 days of lead time for a weather window.

When will I get the photos back? ECS delivers all standard galleries within 24 hours of the shoot. Same-day rush is available when the listing needs to go live the next morning.

I have shot hundreds of listings across Kent County, Ottawa County, and the lakeshore, and the ones that go smoothest share one thing: the agent treated the prep as part of the job, not an afterthought.
