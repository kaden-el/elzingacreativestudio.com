Real Estate

A West Michigan Real Estate Photographer's Seasonal Shoot Calendar

Kaden Elzinga June 16, 2026  ·  8 min read

Every month I shoot real estate in West Michigan, the work changes. Not just what the exteriors look like — though that changes dramatically — but what I prioritize inside, how I schedule twilight, whether drone makes sense, and what I am telling agents to prep before I arrive. I have been shooting listings across Kent County, Ottawa County, and the lakeshore long enough to have a real feel for what each month asks for. This is that calendar, written for agents who want to plan ahead instead of reacting.

January and February: the low-light months

January and February are the darkest months on the West Michigan shoot calendar. Sunrise comes late, sunset comes early, and the window for clean exterior light is narrow. Lake-effect clouds off Lake Michigan can roll in without warning and kill a morning shoot fast. But these months are entirely workable if you lean into them correctly.

The move in deep winter is to make the interior the story. Buyers in January and February are serious. They are not casually browsing — they are tracking the market, and when they see a listing that glows with warm interior light and clean staging, they click. Wide-angle HDR that shows the kitchen, great room, and primary bedroom in even, inviting light carries a listing in this season in a way that nothing else does. The second piece is real twilight. January twilight is actually extraordinary: deep blue sky, warm window glow, clean snow, no leaves to compete with. On the right listing, it is the strongest single image of the year. Drone in January is workable on dry, stable days for showing lot lines and acreage under snow — but it is not the hero.

March: the awkward gap before green-up

March in West Michigan is the most unpredictable month on the calendar. You can get a 50-degree thaw that pulls all the snow off and turns everything mud-brown, or you can get a clean foot of lake-effect in the second week of the month. Neither is ideal for exteriors. The ground is often patchy, the grass is dead, and the trees are bare.

The play in March is to be honest about it and plan accordingly. If a listing can wait three or four weeks for the green-up, that is usually worth it — the difference in exterior appeal between early March and late April is significant. But if the seller needs to go now, the answer is the same as January: invest in the interior and add twilight. A March twilight exterior — especially if there is still clean snow — is far better than a daytime shot of a brown yard. I have shot March listings in Holland that look completely different depending on whether we hit a thaw day or a late snow day; scheduling flexibility helps a lot this month.

April: the green-up window

Late April is one of my favorite months to shoot. The forsythia comes first, then the tulips, then the grass goes green almost overnight. There is a window — usually about two weeks in mid-to-late April — when West Michigan looks as photogenic as it gets: bright green grass, flowering trees, clear air, and long evenings coming back. Listings that hit the market in this window get some of the best exterior conditions of the year for free.

April is also when the spring market starts competing hard. Volume picks up sharply, and a listing that takes a week to get photos back can lose its timing advantage. The 24-hour turnaround matters more in April and May than any other time of year. I keep the rush slot available because I know exactly how competitive the spring list-date race is in Grand Rapids and the suburbs.

May: peak spring, fast market, full calendar

May is the busiest month of the year. The market is hot, inventory moves fast, and I am shooting listings from Rockford to the lakeshore back to back. Everything cooperates in May: the grass is green, the trees are full, the light lasts until 8pm, and drone conditions are usually good. If you have a listing that is ready to go, May is the month to move on.

The one constraint in May is calendar availability. I fill up fast. Agents who book listing shoots a week or two out in April can usually get any slot they want — by mid-May, there are often only a few days open in a given week. If you are planning a May listing, reach out early.

June, July, August: summer peak and the lakeshore window

Summer is the cleanest season to work in. The light is long, the conditions are mostly cooperative, and for lakeshore and waterfront listings, this is the only window that makes the listing look like what buyers are buying. A Lake Michigan frontage property in July — blue water, full trees, the dock in, the kayak on the beach — is a completely different asset than the same property in March. If you have waterfront, summer is non-negotiable.

The drone peak is also summer. Aerial photography over lakeshore towns like Grand Haven, Holland, and Spring Lake in June and July shows the water context that makes those listings worth what they are listed for. Summer drone with a clear sky over a lakefront lot is the highest-impact single image I can produce. I fly under Part 107 RPIC authorization and check LAANC airspace before every shoot, which matters near the Grand Haven Coast Guard Station and the restricted areas around Holland State Park. More on that in the drone laws guide.

The one summer adjustment is timing. Midday in July is harsh: high sun, deep shadows, blown-out skies. I schedule interiors in the midday window and exteriors in the morning or the last two hours before sunset. For most summer listings that means a 10am call time and I come back for exterior and twilight in the evening if the listing warrants it.

September and October: fall color and the timing game

September is still very much a summer-adjacent month in West Michigan. The days are warm, the trees are green, and the light gets better as the sun drops lower. It is a strong month across the board. The shift starts in October, when the maples turn first and the color window opens. A West Michigan home framed in October color — red, orange, gold — is genuinely spectacular if you time it right.

The problem with fall is the window. Peak color in the Grand Rapids area typically lands around the first two weeks of October, and it is driven by temperature and rainfall patterns that vary year to year. A windy rainstorm can strip the trees in 24 hours. The agents who win in fall are the ones who move quickly when the color peaks rather than scheduling weeks out and hoping it holds. I have shot the same neighborhood a week apart and the difference was dramatic. Chase it when it is there.

November and December: back to winter mode

November drops fast in West Michigan. The trees go bare by Halloween in most years, the days shrink, and the lake-effect season starts in earnest. December listings are shooting in effectively full winter conditions. The same playbook as January and February applies: strong interiors, real twilight as the exterior hero, and a fast turnaround because the winter market moves on its own compressed timeline.

One thing that does not change month to month is the 24-hour gallery. Regardless of season, listings get MLS-ready edits back the next day. The season changes what I shoot and how I schedule it. The delivery clock stays the same.

The month-by-month cheat sheet

  • January–February. Invest in interiors + real twilight. Drone for lot lines on stable days. Do not wait on snow — move fast.
  • March. Wait for the green-up if you can. If you cannot, lean on interiors and twilight same as winter.
  • April. Time the green-up, then move immediately. Book early for the 24-hour rush window.
  • May. Best spring conditions. Book two weeks out — calendar fills fast.
  • June–August. Summer peak. Mandatory for waterfront and lakeshore. Peak drone window. Schedule exterior in morning or evening to avoid harsh midday sun.
  • September. Strong across the board. Still summer-adjacent light and conditions.
  • October. Chase peak color in the first two weeks. Move fast before the leaves drop.
  • November–December. Back to winter mode: interiors + twilight. Calendar loosens up; book anytime.
